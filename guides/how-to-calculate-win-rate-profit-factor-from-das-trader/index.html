How to calculate win rate and profit factor from a DAS Trader Pro export

This guide works in any spreadsheet. It turns the DAS Trader Pro Trades window CSV into round-trip trades, then calculates win rate, profit factor, expectancy and R.

1. Export the Trades window

In DAS Trader Pro, right-click the Trades window and export to CSV. You get one row per fill, for example:

Time,Symb,Side,Price,Qty,EcnFees
09:31:05,ABCD,B,4.10,1000,0.30
09:33:40,ABCD,S,4.35,500,0.15
09:35:12,ABCD,S,4.28,500,0.15
10:02:18,WXYZ,SS,12.50,200,0.10
10:07:51,WXYZ,BC,12.70,200,0.10

Fictional example data.

2. Group fills into round trips

Sort by Symb, then Time. Add a signed quantity column: =IF(OR(C2="B",C2="BC"),E2,-E2). Keep a running position per symbol: =IF(B2=B1,G1+F2,F2). A trade ends on the row where the position returns to 0. Give each fill a trade ID that increases each time a new position opens from 0.

3. P&L per trade

Cash flow per fill = -signed qty × price − fees. Sum it by trade ID (SUMIF) for net P&L. In the example, ABCD = −4100 + 2175 + 2140 − 0.60 = +$214.40, and WXYZ = 2500 − 2540 − 0.20 = −$40.20.

4. Win rate

=COUNTIF(PnL,">0")/COUNT(PnL). Decide how breakeven trades count and stay consistent. Example: 1 ÷ 2 = 50%.

5. Profit factor

=SUMIF(PnL,">0")/ABS(SUMIF(PnL,"<0")). Above 1.0 means gross wins exceed gross losses. Example: 214.40 ÷ 40.20 ≈ 5.33. That's two trades, so it means nothing statistically. Use a meaningful sample.

6. Expectancy and average R

Expectancy = win% × avg win − loss% × avg loss (in the example, 0.5 × 214.40 − 0.5 × 40.20 = $87.10 per trade). For R, record your initial stop: risk = |entry − stop| × shares, R = P&L ÷ risk, average R = AVERAGE of R.

7. Max drawdown

Cumulative P&L column, running peak =MAX(peak_prev, cum), drawdown = cum − peak. Max drawdown = MIN of the drawdown column.

Gotchas

  • The Trades window has no date. Add one per export before combining days.
  • Partial exits and scale-ins are why you group by running position rather than pairing rows.
  • Include ECN and commission fees, or your profit factor will look better than it really is.
